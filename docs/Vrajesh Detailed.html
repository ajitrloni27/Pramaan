0 · Read this first — why your seat is load-bearing

The jury never sees the engine. They see your screen rendering it, your 90 seconds proving it, and your voice defending it. Three different failures all land on you:

  • If the screen drifts from the engine (schemas, shapes), the P3 merge breaks silently and the sprint burns — that’s why you build against a MOCK from P1 and treat the schema as a contract.
  • If the demo isn’t deterministic, the stage breaks — that’s why the 90 seconds run on seeded data and every rehearsal is filmed.
  • If the team doesn’t stay one team, four lanes become four solo projects — that’s why the all-hands, the two daily V–M syncs, the sleep call, and the cut decisions are YOURS.

You are not “the frontend guy.” You are the person who converts a correct engine into a winning demonstration — and the only person whose job is also the team itself.

1 · Your role — one line, and in the tree

One line: You build the bark — the screen that renders the engine’s truth verbatim, the demo that proves it deterministically, the pitch that sells it, and the coordination that keeps four people one team.

In the tree: the bark is what the world touches. The trunk (Murgesh) carries the verdict; the sap (Ajit) feeds the reads and the words; the roots (Manas) hold the cited ground. The bark protects all of it and makes it legible. One screen, one demo, one voice — that’s your branch of the tree.

2 · The co-lead contract — your side of it

  • You hold the FINAL CALL on direction, product, the screen, and the pitch. Murgesh owns the engine implementation — the HOW.
  • You WANT his push-back. Before he builds something you specced, if he thinks the call is wrong, he says so. On technical-implementation disagreements his view carries real weight. That is your safety net, not insubordination.
  • Delegate with the WHY. Murgesh executes faster and better when he understands the reasoning. If he asks “why,” the system is working — answer it.
  • Once aligned, the team commits. No re-litigating after the decision.
  • Two daily V–M syncs (phase start + mid, 10 min each) — for ALIGNMENT, not negotiation. Keep engine + screen in lockstep.
  • Schema-first (rule #1): Murgesh freezes the 3 schemas at P1 10:30; you build against a mock from that moment. A schema change needs a 2-min V–M sync, never a surprise. If drift is found late, the UI bends to the engine — UI is cheaper to bend than verdict logic.
  • You are the sole merger of the app. Murgesh is the sole merger of services/brain; packages/contracts needs both of you.

13 · ⚠ The ten non-negotiables — you ENFORCE these

  • 1 · Schema-first. Schemas freeze P1; you build against mock; changes need a 2-min V–M sync, never a surprise. If drift is found late, the UI bends to the engine.
  • 2 · One data source, one switch. Everything behind dataSource (mock | live). Flip one env var at P3. No find-and-replace across the UI.
  • 3 · DRI. Your lane, your decision; others advise, never override. A committed wrong call beats an uncommitted right one.
  • 4 · Two alphas, two syncs. You and Murgesh, 10 min, twice a build-day. No more, no less.
  • 5 · Citation gate. No rule, no on-screen number, no slide stat ships without Manas’s sign-off. The team waits 10 minutes for a correct citation rather than demo a wrong number.
  • 6 · Deterministic demo. Pre-seeded data that looks live. No live OCR/LLM/network in the 90 seconds. The demo is a rehearsed script.
  • 7 · Last 4 hours = Ajit. No new features. Only breaking and fixing. A flawless demo of 80% beats 100% that glitches.
  • 8 · Sleep both nights. All four. The all-nighter writes the bug that breaks the 11 AM demo.
  • 9 · One voice per question. The owner answers; others stay quiet unless tagged. Four people talking = a team that didn’t rehearse.
  • 10 · The tree stays a tree. The lease is a rulebook swap on the SAME screen. Copy-pasting the engine = amputating the tree. Stop it.
