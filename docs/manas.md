## 1. What you are actually making

You are making a LIST. That's it. A list in a file.

Each line in the list says ONE thing:

"For THIS item on a bill or lease, the CORRECT price or rule is THIS number,
and I got that number from THIS official document."

The computer reads your list. It looks at what the person was ACTUALLY
charged. It subtracts. If the charged price is higher than your number,
it shows a red card: "You were charged more than the law allows. Here's
the proof."

Your list IS the proof. Without your list, the computer has nothing to
compare against. You are the person who knows the right answer.

---

## 2. What goes in each line of your list

Every line has exactly 4 things. Nothing more.

1. THE NAME — what the item is called on the bill or in the lease.
   Example: "MRI Brain Scan" or "Security Deposit clause"

2. THE NUMBER — the official correct price or the rule.
   Example: "Rs 4,500 per scan" or "Deposit must be refundable"

3. THE SOURCE — where you got that number. Which document. Which page
   or section. Which year.
   Example: "CGHS Rate Card 2024, page 12, Entry 214"
   Example: "Model Tenancy Act 2021, Section 11"

4. A SIMPLE SENTENCE — one line a normal person can read and understand.
   Example: "The government says an MRI Brain Scan should not cost more
   than Rs 4,500. If they charged you more, you were overcharged."

That's it. Name. Number. Source. Simple sentence. Four things per line.

## 4. Where to find the numbers (exact places to look)

### Hospital procedure prices:

Search this on Google: "CGHS rate card 2024 PDF" or
"CGHS procedure rates list"

CGHS = Central Government Health Scheme. The government publishes a
price list that says the MAXIMUM a hospital can charge for each
procedure. It's a public PDF. Open it. Find the procedure. Write down
the number and the page/entry number. That's your source.

If you can't find CGHS for a specific item, search:
"[state name] health department procedure rates 2025"
or "AIIMS procedure charges list" (AIIMS publishes its rates publicly).

### Medicine prices:

The easiest source: LOOK AT THE MEDICINE STRIP. The MRP printed on the
strip IS the legal maximum price. No one can charge more than MRP.
So: pick up a strip of Paracetamol. Read the MRP. That's your number.
Your source is: "MRP printed on strip / Drug Price Control Order."

For a more official source, search: "NPPA scheduled drug prices list"
or "DPCO 2013 price list PDF." NPPA = the government body that fixes
medicine prices.

### Rental law:

Search: "Model Tenancy Act 2021 full text PDF"

It's a government document, freely available. Open it. Look for:
- Section 11 (security deposit — must be refundable, capped)
- Section 10 (landlord entry — needs 24 hours notice)
- Section 12 (lock-in — must have fair exit terms)
- Section 15 (rent increase — must follow agreed terms)

Read the section. Write down what it says in YOUR words (simple words).
Write down the section number. That's your source.

For state-specific rules, search: "[state name] rent control rules 2025"
or "[state name] new rental rules 2026." Some states have their own
caps on deposits and rent increases.

## 5. How to do it, day by day (your method)

Do ONE rule at a time. Don't try to do all 15 in one sitting.

For each rule, follow these 5 steps:

Step 1: Pick the item. (Example: "MRI Brain Scan")

Step 2: Find the official source. (Search CGHS rate card. Open the PDF.
Find "MRI Brain." Note the price and the entry number.)

Step 3: Write down the number. (Example: Rs 4,500 per scan.)

Step 4: Write down the source. (Example: "CGHS Rate Card 2024,
Schedule II, Entry 214.")

Step 5: Write the simple sentence. (Example: "The government says an
MRI Brain Scan should not cost more than Rs 4,500. If you were charged
more, you were overcharged.")

Done. That's one line. Move to the next item.

Do 2-3 lines per day this week. By 31 July, you'll have 10 hospital
rules done. By 7 August, add the 5 lease rules. That's your full list.

## 6. The golden rule (the most important thing)

If you CANNOT find a clean official source for a number:

DO NOT make up a number.
DO NOT guess.
DO NOT write "approximately Rs 5,000" if you're not sure.

Instead, write: "SOURCE NOT FOUND" in the source column, and tell
Vrajesh: "I couldn't find a clean source for this one. The card will
say 'check with a person' instead of showing a number."

That is the CORRECT thing to do. A card that says "we're not sure, ask
a human" is a HONEST card. A card that shows a made-up number is a LIE.
And our whole product is built on "prove, don't guess." You are the
person who makes that promise real. So when you're not sure, you say
so. That's not failure. That's the job.
