## BRANCH 2: RENTAL AGREEMENT (you already have this one)

The "official rule" here is a LAW CLAUSE — what the tenancy law says
is allowed or not allowed in a lease.

Worked example (Non-refundable deposit):
  Name:    "Security deposit is non-refundable" clause
  Number:  ILLEGAL — deposit must be refundable
  Source:  Model Tenancy Act 2021, Section 11
  Simple:  "The law says your security deposit MUST be refundable.
           A clause that says 'non-refundable' is not enforceable.
           You do not have to accept it."

Three more rows to add by 7 Aug:

  Name:    "Landlord can enter the flat anytime without notice"
  Number:  ILLEGAL — landlord must give 24 hours written notice
  Source:  Model Tenancy Act 2021, Section 10
  Simple:  "Your landlord cannot walk into your flat whenever they want.
           The law says they must give you 24 hours notice in writing.
           A clause that says 'anytime entry' is illegal."

  Name:    "6-month lock-in, no early exit allowed"
  Number:  RISKY — lock-in must have fair exit terms
  Source:  Model Tenancy Act 2021, Section 12
  Simple:  "A lock-in period is allowed, but it must have fair terms
           for leaving early (like 1 month notice). A clause that says
           'no exit for 6 months, period' is not fair and can be challenged."

  Name:    "Tenant pays for ALL repairs including structural"
  Number:  ILLEGAL — structural repairs are the landlord's job
  Source:  Model Tenancy Act 2021, Section 13
  Simple:  "You are responsible for small fixes (like a tap). But big
           structural repairs (roof, walls, plumbing) are the landlord's
           job by law. A clause that makes you pay for everything is illegal."

## BRANCH 3: GIG WORKER PAYSLIP (new — this is how it looks)

NOTE: The "official rule" here is NOT a government price list. It is
the PLATFORM'S OWN PUBLISHED RATE CARD (what they promise to pay) PLUS
the government's MINIMUM WAGE law. You are comparing what the platform
PROMISED vs what they PAID. Their own document is the evidence against them.

Worked example (Base pay per delivery):
  Name:    Base pay per delivery (Swiggy/Zomato, Bengaluru)
  Number:  Rs 15 per delivery (base, before distance/bonus)
  Source:  Platform partner rate card, published [month/year], [URL or screenshot]
  Simple:  "The platform says you earn Rs 15 per delivery as base pay.
           If your payslip shows Rs 12, you were underpaid Rs 3 per delivery.
           Over 22 deliveries, that is Rs 66 stolen from your week."

Four more rows to add:

  Name:    Distance pay per km (after first 2 km)
  Number:  Rs 6 per km
  Source:  Platform partner rate card, distance section
  Simple:  "You should earn Rs 6 for every km after the first 2 km.
           Multiply your total km minus 2 by Rs 6. If your payslip shows
           less, they short-changed you on distance."

  Name:    Peak hour bonus (lunch 12-3 PM, dinner 7-10 PM)
  Number:  Rs 40 extra per delivery during peak hours
  Source:  Platform partner rate card, peak/bonus section
  Simple:  "Deliveries during lunch (12-3 PM) and dinner (7-10 PM) earn
           an extra Rs 40 each. Count your peak-hour deliveries and multiply
           by Rs 40. If that bonus is missing from your payslip, it was not paid."

  Name:    Daily minimum wage (Karnataka, unskilled worker)
  Number:  Rs 500 per day (8-hour shift)
  Source:  Karnataka Minimum Wages Notification, 2024
  Simple:  "By law, no worker in Karnataka can be paid less than Rs 500
           for a full day's work. If you worked 10 hours and earned Rs 487,
           you were paid BELOW the legal minimum. That is wage theft."

  Name:    Maximum platform deduction (fee cap)
  Number:  Platform fee cannot exceed 20% of gross earnings
  Source:  Platform terms of service / Code on Social Security 2020
  Simple:  "The platform can take a fee, but not more than 20% of what
           you earned. If your gross was Rs 1,000 and they deducted Rs 300,
           that is 30% — they over-deducted by Rs 100."

HOW TO FIND THE RATE CARD:
- Open the Swiggy/Zomato/Uber partner app or website
- Look for "earnings," "rate card," "payout structure," or "partner terms"
- Screenshot it. Save the URL. Note the date it was published.
- That screenshot IS your source. The platform's own promise is the evidence.
- For minimum wage: search "Karnataka minimum wages notification 2024 PDF"
  (or whichever state). It is a government PDF, freely available.

## BRANCH 4: MEDICINE STRIP (new — this is how it looks)

NOTE: The "official rule" here is NOT a price (except for MRP). It is a
SAFETY STATUS — is this batch safe to take? The source is the drug
regulator's (CDSCO) public recall and "Not of Standard Quality" list.
You are NOT guessing if a medicine is fake. You are checking the batch
number against the regulator's OWN published list. If it is on the list,
it is unsafe. If it is not, you say "not on the recall list" — you do
NOT say "verified safe" (because you cannot verify safety from a photo).

Worked example (Recalled batch):
  Name:    [Brand] [Composition], Batch #AXM-2024-7742
  Number:  RECALLED — do not dispense / do not consume
  Source:  CDSCO Drug Alert, January 2026, Alert #12, [URL]
  Simple:  "This exact batch was recalled by India's drug regulator (CDSCO)
           in January 2026. It may be unsafe. Do not consume it. Return it
           to the pharmacy and ask for a different batch."

Four more rows to add:

  Name:    [Brand] [Composition], Batch #XYZ-2025-3391
  Number:  NOT OF STANDARD QUALITY (NSQ) — failed lab testing
  Source:  CDSCO NSQ Report, June 2026, Entry 47, [URL]
  Simple:  "This batch failed quality testing by a government drug lab.
           It may not work as intended. Do not consume. Ask for a different batch."

  Name:    Any medicine — MRP check
  Number:  MRP printed on strip = legal maximum price
  Source:  DPCO 2013 / MRP on strip
  Simple:  "No pharmacy can charge you more than the MRP printed on the strip.
           If they did, you were overcharged. The MRP is the law."

  Name:    Any medicine — Expiry check
  Number:  Must not be sold after the expiry date on the strip
  Source:  Drugs & Cosmetics Act, Rule 65
  Simple:  "A pharmacy cannot sell you medicine past its expiry date.
           Check the date on the strip. If it is expired, do not take it
           and report the pharmacy."

  Name:    Any medicine — Composition match
  Number:  Composition on strip must match the doctor's prescription
  Source:  Prescription vs strip comparison (user provides both)
  Simple:  "The medicine you got should have the same composition (salt)
           as what the doctor wrote. If the strip says a different salt,
           it is the wrong medicine. Do not take it."

HOW TO FIND THE RECALL / NSQ LIST:
- Search: "CDSCO drug alert 2026" or "CDSCO NSQ report 2026"
- CDSCO publishes these monthly on their website (cdsco.gov.in)
- Each alert lists: drug name, batch number, manufacturer, reason, date
- You copy the batch number + the alert reference into your rulebook row
- For the sprint: pick 3-5 batches from the most recent 2-3 alerts
- You do NOT need to check every medicine ever made. You check the ones
  on the regulator's list. That is the rulebook. The list IS the rule.

IMPORTANT: You are NOT detecting fakes from a photo. You are LOOKING UP
a batch number against a published list. That is a lookup, not a guess.
If the batch is not on the list, the card says "not on recall list —
confirm with pharmacist." It does NOT say "verified safe." You cannot
verify safety from a photo. You can only check the list. That is the
precision-over-recall rule. Silence over a false alarm.

## BRANCH 5: TRAFFIC CHALLAN (new — this is how it looks)

NOTE: The "official rule" here is a FINE SCHEDULE — what the Motor
Vehicles Act says the fine should be for each offence — PLUS procedural
rules (what a valid challan must include). You are checking: is the fine
amount correct? Is the section correct? Was the procedure followed?

Worked example (Wrong fine amount):
  Name:    Over-speeding (car, first offence)
  Number:  Rs 1,000 (NOT Rs 2,000)
  Source:  Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Section 183 (as amended 2019)
  Simple:  "The fine for over-speeding a car (first time) is Rs 1,000
           by law. If your challan says Rs 2,000, they charged you double
           the legal fine. You can contest it."

Four more rows to add:

  Name:    Not wearing seatbelt (driver)
  Number:  Rs 1,000, Section 138
  Source:  Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Section 138 (as amended 2019)
  Simple:  "Not wearing a seatbelt is Section 138, fine Rs 1,000.
           If your challan cites a different section or a higher fine,
           the challan is wrong."

  Name:    Any e-challan — must include photo/video evidence
  Number:  A valid e-challan MUST show a photo or video of the violation
  Source:  MoRTH e-Challan Guidelines, 2023
  Simple:  "An e-challan must show a photo or video of what you did wrong.
           If there is no photo, no video, no evidence — you can contest
           the challan. No evidence, no fine."

  Name:    Any e-challan — vehicle number must match
  Number:  Vehicle number on challan must match your registration plate
  Source:  MoRTH e-Challan Guidelines / basic procedural fairness
  Simple:  "Check the vehicle number on the challan. If it does not match
           your number plate, the challan is for someone else's car.
           It is not yours. You do not have to pay it."

  Name:    Double fine for same violation
  Number:  Cannot be fined twice for the same violation, same day, same spot
  Source:  Legal principle against double penalty / Motor Vehicles Act
  Simple:  "You cannot be fined twice for the same thing on the same day
           at the same spot. If you got two challans for the same time and
           place, one of them is invalid. Contest the duplicate."

HOW TO FIND THE FINE SCHEDULE:
- Search: "Motor Vehicles Act 1988 fine schedule amended 2019 PDF"
  or "traffic fine list India 2025"
- The 2019 amendment changed most fines. Make sure you use the CURRENT
  (post-2019) amounts, not the old ones.
- For procedural rules: search "MoRTH e-challan guidelines 2023"
- For state-specific fines: some states have their own notifications.
  Search "[state name] traffic fine notification 2025"
- The fine schedule is a TABLE: offence name | section | fine amount.
  You copy the relevant rows into your rulebook. That is the rule.

## THE PATTERN (same in every branch)

Every branch, every row, is the same 4 things:

1. NAME — what the item is called (on the bill, in the lease, on the
   payslip, on the strip, on the challan)

2. NUMBER — the official correct value. But "value" means something
   different in each branch:
   - Hospital bill: the official PRICE (CGHS rate)
   - Lease: the legal STATUS (illegal / risky / allowed)
   - Gig payslip: the platform's PROMISED RATE + legal minimum wage
   - Medicine strip: the SAFETY STATUS (recalled / NSQ / not on list)
   - Traffic challan: the legal FINE AMOUNT + procedural rule

3. SOURCE — where you got that number. Always an official document:
   - CGHS rate card / DPCO / MRP (hospital)
   - Model Tenancy Act / state rent rules (lease)
   - Platform rate card / Minimum Wages Notification (gig)
   - CDSCO alert / NSQ report (medicine)
   - Motor Vehicles Act / MoRTH guidelines (traffic)

4. SIMPLE SENTENCE — one line a normal person reads and understands.
   No jargon. No section numbers in the sentence (those go in the source).
   Just: "This is what's right. This is what happened to you. Here's the gap."

The engine does the same job every time: read your paper, look up the
official rule, compare, show the gap. You just change which rulebook
it reads. That is the tree. You build the roots once per branch. The
trunk never changes.
